### Phase 1: Claim Verification

**Claim 1: Application intake uses Google Forms + manual spreadsheet tracking**
- **Response:** ✅ Confirmed
- "Yes, we use Google Forms for the initial application. But after that, everything goes into a spreadsheet — we manually encode the responses from the Google Form into our Excel tracking sheet. Sometimes the Google Form responses don't match what applicants actually submit physically, so we have to double-check." `[SIM-REG-01]`
- **Note:** Partially confirmed — Google Forms exists but the workflow is not fully digital; there's a manual re-encoding step.

**Claim 2: Registrar manually generates admission slips using Word templates**
- **Response:** ✅ Confirmed
- "Yes, we use a Word template for the admission slip. We copy the applicant's name and details from the spreadsheet into the template, print it, then have it signed. Each slip takes maybe 2-3 minutes, but when you have hundreds of applicants, that adds up." `[SIM-REG-02]`

**Claim 3: There is no automated status notification to applicants**
- **Response:** ✅ Confirmed
- "No, there's nothing automatic. Applicants have to come back here or call us to check their status. Sometimes they text, sometimes they show up in person. During peak season, we get a lot of people asking 'what's my status?' and we have to look it up one by one." `[SIM-REG-03]`

**Claim 4: Exam scheduling is coordinated verbally with Guidance**
- **Response:** ⚠️ Partially confirmed
- "It's verbal and through text messages, yes. Sometimes email. There's no formal system for it. I message the Guidance Office and say 'we have this many applicants ready for testing, can we schedule a date?' and then we go from there. If the Guidance staff is busy or there's a miscommunication, the scheduling gets delayed." `[SIM-REG-04]`
- **Note:** Confirmed verbal coordination, but text messaging is also used. No formal system.

**Claim 5: Applicant documents stored in physical folders with no digital backup**
- **Response:** ⚠️ Partially confirmed
- "We have physical folders, yes — organized by academic year and program. The Google Form has some digital records, but the actual documents like grades, birth certificates, photos — those are all physical. We don't scan them. If something gets misplaced, we have to ask the applicant to resubmit." `[SIM-REG-05]`
- **Note:** Partial digital exists via Google Form responses, but supporting documents are physical-only. No systematic digital backup.

### Phase 2: Process Walk-Through

**"Walk me through what happens from when an applicant submits their application to when they receive their admission result — step by step."**

> "Okay, so first the applicant comes to our office or hears about the admission through Facebook or word of mouth. They fill out the Google Form online — or sometimes we help them fill it here in the office if they don't have internet or a phone. After that, they need to submit physical requirements: Form 138, birth certificate, ID photos, certificate of good moral character, and their GWA or transcript. `[SIM-REG-06]`
>
> We check if the documents are complete. If not, we tell them what's missing and they have to come back. If complete, we encode their info into our spreadsheet and give them a reference number. Then we generate the admission slip — that's the Word template I mentioned. We print it, sign it, and give it to the applicant so they can proceed to the Guidance Office for the exam. `[SIM-REG-07]`
>
> The scheduling part — we coordinate with Guidance on when the exam will be. Usually there are set dates. During peak season, maybe once a week or every two weeks. After the exam, Guidance scores it and sends us the results. We then inform the applicants — usually they come back to check, or we post a list on the bulletin board. The final admission decision is made by the Director based on the exam results and the applicant's GWA, and then we process the enrollment." `[SIM-REG-08]`

**"At which step does Registrar's responsibility end and Guidance's begin?"**
> "Our job is intake, document verification, and issuing the admission slip. Once we give them the slip and they go to Guidance for the exam, that's where our direct involvement pauses. After the exam results come back, we handle the enrollment processing. So there's a handoff — we pass the applicant to Guidance, and Guidance passes the results back to us." `[SIM-REG-09]`

**"How many applicants per cycle? Per day at peak?"**
> "It depends on the semester. For the main admission cycle, maybe around 300 to 400 applicants. During peak days, maybe 30 to 50 applicants come in per day. But it's not evenly spread — there are days when nobody comes and then suddenly a rush." `[SIM-REG-10]`
- **STEER RESOLVED:** Annual applicant volume ≈ 300-400 per cycle. Peak days: 30-50 applicants/day.

**"What forms/documents do you generate?"**
> "We generate the admission slip, the applicant tracking spreadsheet, and sometimes a summary report for the Director showing how many applicants per program. The admission slip is the main document." `[SIM-REG-11]`

**"What happens when documents are incomplete?"**
> "We tell them what's missing and ask them to come back. Sometimes they travel from far municipalities, so it's a burden. We can't process them without complete documents. Some applicants give up at that point." `[SIM-REG-12]`

**"Is the final decision to accept an admission the responsibility of the Registrar?"**
> "No, not mine. The Campus Director makes the final decision, based on the exam results and other criteria. We just process whatever is approved." `[SIM-REG-13]`

**"Who holds the primary responsibility for scheduling examinations — your office or Guidance?"**
> "It's a joint thing. We tell Guidance how many applicants are ready, and they set the schedule based on room availability and their capacity. But Guidance has the final say on exam dates since they handle the actual testing." `[SIM-REG-14]`

### Phase 3: Pain Points

**"What are the top 3 things that slow you down or frustrate you in the current admission process?"**

> "Number one is the manual encoding. Transferring data from Google Forms to our spreadsheet, then to the Word template — it's repetitive and we sometimes make typos. Number two is tracking applicant status. We have to remember or manually look up where each applicant is in the process. Did they take the exam already? Did they pass? It's all in our heads or the spreadsheet, which isn't always updated. Number three is the communication. Telling applicants their status one by one — whether by phone, text, or when they show up — takes a lot of our time during peak season." `[SIM-REG-15]`

**"What do you do manually that you wish a system handled?"**
> "Definitely the admission slip generation — if applicants could fill their own data online and the system auto-generates the slip, that would save us hours. Also the status tracking — if applicants could check their own status online instead of calling or coming here, that would be a big relief." `[SIM-REG-16]`

**"Do you use any part of a digital system? Which parts?"**
> "Just the Google Form for initial application and the Excel spreadsheet for tracking. Everything else is manual — Word, printing, physical folders." `[SIM-REG-17]`

**"Have you ever lost data, had duplicates, or campus mix-ups?"**
> "Yes, sometimes we get duplicate entries in the spreadsheet when someone is encoded twice. And there have been times when an applicant's file was misplaced — then we have to ask them to resubmit. No campus mix-ups since we're just one campus handling our own, but I could see that being a problem if there were multiple campuses involved." `[SIM-REG-18]`

**"How is applicant data protected? Who can access the spreadsheets?"**
> "The spreadsheet is on my computer. I have a password on my account, but the file itself isn't password-protected. The physical folders are in our filing cabinet, which is locked after office hours. Only our staff access the records. I don't think we have a formal data privacy protocol beyond that." `[SIM-REG-19]`

**"How does the office currently handle system processes during offline periods or internet outages?"**
> "If the internet is down, we can't access the Google Form responses. So we just work with what we have on the spreadsheet and ask applicants to come back later for the online part. Internet outages happen, especially during rainy season. Maybe once or twice a week for a few hours." `[SIM-REG-20]`

---

### Phase 4: System Integration

**"If a unified digital system could auto-generate admission slips from applicant-filled web forms, how would that change your workload?"**
> "That would be a huge help. If the applicant enters their own data and the system generates the slip, I just review and approve it. That probably cuts our processing time in half for each applicant." `[SIM-REG-21]`

**"Would you be open to applicants filling their own data online?"**
> "Yes, as long as we can verify the documents they upload. Some applicants might put wrong information, so we need a review step." `[SIM-REG-22]`

**"Do you need to work offline? How often does internet go down?"**
> "Yes, we need some offline capability. The internet goes down maybe once or twice a week during the rainy season, sometimes for the whole afternoon. If the system works offline and syncs when the connection comes back, that would be ideal." `[SIM-REG-23]`

**"What reports does the Director ask for? How long does that take?"**
> "The Director asks for a summary of applicants per program, how many have taken the exam, and how many have been accepted. I compile that from the spreadsheet — it takes maybe half a day to put together a proper report." `[SIM-REG-24]`

**"Is the office willing to integrate with an automated exam system that imports applicant data directly upon review?"**
> "Yes, if it means we don't have to manually coordinate with Guidance about who's ready for the exam. If the system knows who has complete documents and is cleared for testing, that would make everything smoother." `[SIM-REG-25]`

**"How feasible is a transition to online document uploading for grades and forms?"**
> "It's feasible for the younger applicants — they're comfortable with uploading files. But some applicants from remote areas might have trouble. A hybrid approach where they can either upload online or submit physical copies would be best." `[SIM-REG-26]`

### Phase 1: Claim Verification

**Claim 2: Guidance staff handle all proctoring rather than delegating to other faculty**
- **Response:** ✅ Confirmed
- "Yes, it's just us. During exam days, we proctor all the rooms ourselves. Sometimes it's overwhelming — if we have three rooms running simultaneously, we're stretched thin. We've asked for help from other faculty before, but there's no formal arrangement." `[SIM-GUID-02]`

**Claim 3: Attendance during exams is tracked on paper sign-in sheets**
- **Response:** ✅ Confirmed
- "Yes, we use a printed attendance sheet. The examinees sign in when they arrive. We check IDs against the list from the Registrar. It works but it's another manual step." `[SIM-GUID-03]`

**Claim 4: Test results computed in Excel and manually transferred to Registrar**
- **Response:** ✅ Confirmed
- "After scoring, I encode the results into an Excel file with the applicant's name, score, and recommended course. Then I either print it or send the file to the Registrar. Sometimes there are transcription errors when copying scores from the answer sheets to Excel." `[SIM-GUID-04]`

**Claim 5: No system-level audit trail for who accessed or modified scores**
- **Response:** ✅ Confirmed
- "No, there's no audit trail. The Excel file is on my computer. If I change a score, there's no record of the change. It's based on trust. We know it's not ideal, but we don't have a system that tracks modifications." `[SIM-GUID-05]`

### Phase 2: Testing Workflow

**"Walk me through exam day — from room setup to score release."**

> "Exam day starts early. I come in around 7 AM to prepare the rooms — arrange chairs, print the attendance sheets, make sure we have enough answer sheets and test booklets printed. The test is a standardized aptitude test covering English, Math, Science, and Abstract Reasoning. `[SIM-GUID-06]`
>
> Applicants arrive and we verify them against the Registrar's list. They sign the attendance sheet. Then we distribute the test materials and give instructions. The exam takes about 2-3 hours depending on the number of sections. I proctor — I walk around, watch for cheating, answer questions about instructions but not the actual items. `[SIM-GUID-07]`
>
> After the exam, I collect all the answer sheets. Scoring happens over the next few days — I go through each sheet one by one, comparing to the answer key. For a batch of 50 applicants, scoring takes about 2-3 days working on it between other tasks. Then I compute the raw scores, apply the conversion table for the rating scale, and determine the recommended course based on the score brackets. `[SIM-GUID-08]`
>
> Once I have all the results, I compile them in Excel and send them to the Registrar and the Director. The applicants usually come back after a week or two to check their results. We don't have a way for them to check online." `[SIM-GUID-09]`

**"If a system allowed other staff to proctor securely, would that help?"**
> "Yes, definitely. If we could assign proctors from other departments and the system controlled what they can access — like they can only see the examinees in their room and can't modify scores — that would help a lot during peak testing days." `[SIM-GUID-10]`

**"How do you coordinate exam schedule/rooms with Registrar?"**
> "Usually through text or in person. The Registrar tells me how many applicants are ready, and I set the date based on room availability and my schedule. There's no shared calendar or system for this." `[SIM-GUID-11]`

**"What if a staff member is absent on a heavy testing day?"**
> "It's a problem. I've had to reschedule exams before because I was the only one available. If there's a scheduling conflict or someone gets sick, the applicants are the ones who suffer — they have to come back another day." `[SIM-GUID-12]`

**"How long from last exam to result release?"**
> "About one to two weeks, depending on how many applicants we need to score and how busy we are with other guidance tasks. During peak season, it can take up to two weeks." `[SIM-GUID-13]`
- **STEER RESOLVED:** Processing time from exam to result release: 1-2 weeks.

**"Have there been scoring disputes or errors?"**
> "Occasionally, yes. Sometimes an applicant questions their score, and we have to manually recheck their answer sheet. There have been cases where we found a scoring error — a miscounted item or a wrong entry in the Excel file. It doesn't happen often, but when it does, it's embarrassing and we have to correct it." `[SIM-GUID-14]`

**"What are the specific frustrations you have experienced with the current admission testing process and any tools or methods you have used in the past?"**
> "The biggest frustration is the scoring. Doing it manually for hundreds of answer sheets is tedious and error-prone. I've tried using Excel formulas to speed it up, but I still have to manually enter each answer for each applicant. Also, generating the course recommendations — I have to cross-reference the scores against the program quotas manually. It would be great if the system could handle both the scoring and the recommendation logic." `[SIM-GUID-15]`

---

### Phase 3: Pain Points + Security

**"What's the most stressful part of the admission testing season?"**

> "The volume. When we have a big batch of applicants and a short timeframe to score and release results, the pressure is intense. I'm doing scoring after office hours, during weekends, just to keep up. And knowing that a single scoring error could affect someone's admission — that weighs on me." `[SIM-GUID-16]`

**"Unauthorized score access or tampering incidents?"**
> "Not that I'm aware of, but the concern is valid. The Excel file is on my computer, and while I trust my colleagues, there's no technical safeguard preventing someone from opening the file and changing a score. If that happened, we'd have no way of knowing." `[SIM-GUID-17]`

**"What happens when Wi-Fi fails during testing?"**
> "If the internet goes down during testing, it doesn't affect the paper exam directly, since everything is on paper. But it does affect our coordination — we can't email or message the Registrar about no-shows or issues. After the exam, if the internet is down, I can't send the results digitally and have to wait or print them." `[SIM-GUID-18]`

**"How do you handle special accommodations?"**
> "We've had a few cases — applicants with disabilities or special needs. We try to accommodate them, like giving extra time or a separate room. But there's no formal process in place. It's handled case by case." `[SIM-GUID-19]`

**"How do you manage course recommendations with quotas?"**
> "The Director gives us the program quotas — how many students per course. Based on the exam scores and the applicant's GWA, we recommend a course. But if a course is already full, we have to recommend an alternative. It's a manual matching process — I check the quota list and the applicant's score bracket to determine the recommendation." `[SIM-GUID-20]`

---

### Phase 4: Requirements + Advanced

**"If you could change one thing about testing and scoring, what would it be?"**
> "Automated scoring. If I could scan or photograph the answer sheets and have a system score them instantly, that would be the single biggest improvement. It would cut days off our processing time." `[SIM-GUID-21]`

**"Would automated OMR scanning be a priority?"**
> "Yes, absolutely. If the technology exists where we can scan answer sheets and the system reads the answers and scores them automatically, that would be our top priority. We'd need a way to print the answer sheets in the right format, but that's manageable." `[SIM-GUID-22]`

**"Would both auto-computation AND your conversion tables future-proof scoring?"**
> "Yes, that makes sense. The system should compute the raw score automatically, but also apply our conversion table — the rating scale we use to convert raw scores to the standardized rating. If both are built in, it covers our current process and any future changes to the scale." `[SIM-GUID-23]`

**"If an AI companion were available, how could it assist you?"**
> "An AI chatbot that applicants could ask about the admission process — like what documents they need, when the next exam date is, what their status is — that would reduce the number of people coming to our office just to ask questions. As for us, if the AI could help analyze trends in scores or flag unusual patterns, that could be useful for reporting to the Director." `[SIM-GUID-24]`

**"Would you support a system that records and forecasts recommended courses for students to track enrollment trends and quotas?"**
> "Yes, that would be very helpful. Right now, I manually check quotas against recommendations. If the system could show me in real-time how many slots are left per program and which applicants to prioritize, that would streamline the whole recommendation process." `[SIM-GUID-25]`

### 8 Strategic Questions

**Q1: Digital vision — "How fully digital do you envision the Tagudin admission experience becoming?"**
> "I want it as fully digital as possible. The pandemic showed us that we need to be able to operate without requiring applicants to physically come to the campus for every step. Ideally, applicants should be able to apply, check their status, and receive results online. The exam itself may still need to be on-site for security, but everything around it should be digital." `[SIM-DIR-01]`

**Q2: Infrastructure — "Would you prefer local MIS servers or cloud hosting for uptime, given seasonal usage?"**
> "Cloud hosting would be ideal for reliability, but given our internet situation, we need the system to work even when the connection is unstable. If there's a way to have both — cloud with offline capability — that would be best. Budget is a consideration, so whatever minimizes cost while maintaining reliability." `[SIM-DIR-02]`

**Q3: Multi-campus — "How do you feel about a unified system allowing seamless applicant transfers across ISPSC campuses?"**
> "That would be excellent in the long term. Right now each campus handles their own admission independently. If an applicant from Tagudin wants to transfer to another campus, it's a manual process. A unified system would streamline that, but it would need coordination at the college level, not just our campus. For now, focus on Tagudin but design with multi-campus in mind." `[SIM-DIR-03]`

**Q4: Dashboard — "Would you prefer a live admin dashboard to monitor admission progress, or generated reports from staff?"**
> "A live dashboard would be much better. Right now, I request reports from the Registrar, and it takes time to compile. If I could log in and see how many applicants we have per program, how many have tested, how many have been accepted — in real-time — that would improve my ability to make timely decisions." `[SIM-DIR-04]`

**Q5: Proctor delegation — "Would you support delegating proctoring to non-Guidance staff with role-based access ensuring test security?"**
> "Yes, I support that, as long as there are proper access controls. Not everyone should see everything. If the system can restrict what a proctor sees — just the examinees in their room and the exam instructions — that's sufficient. It would free up Guidance staff to focus on scoring and counseling." `[SIM-DIR-05]`

**Q6: Data privacy & system security — "What are your expectations regarding RA 10173 compliance and system security for applicant records?"**
> "Full compliance is non-negotiable. We collect personal data from applicants, including minors in some cases. The system must protect that data — proper access controls, audit trails, and secure storage. I expect the system to log who accessed what and when. Data privacy is a legal requirement under RA 10173 and we take it seriously." `[SIM-DIR-06]`

**Q7: AI companion support — "Would you support an AI companion or chatbot system to assist examinees with routine inquiries during the admission process?"**
> "Yes, that's a welcome addition. Many applicants have the same basic questions — requirements, schedule, how to check status. If an AI can handle those, it reduces the burden on our staff. It should be clear that it's an AI assistant, not a human, and it should only provide factual information, not advice on which course to choose." `[SIM-DIR-07]`

**Q8: Course quotas & enrollment targets — "How should course quotas and enrollment targets be managed within the admission system?"**
> "The system should allow me or my designated admin to set the quotas per program at the start of each admission cycle. During processing, it should track how many slots are filled versus how many are remaining. When a program is full, the system should flag it so that Guidance can recommend alternatives. Historical data on enrollment trends would also be valuable for planning future quotas." `[SIM-DIR-08]`
