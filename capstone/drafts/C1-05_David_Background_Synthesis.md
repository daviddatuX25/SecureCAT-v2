# Background of the Study

## Paragraph 5 — Synthesis and Gap Identification

[indent]

The literature reviewed above and the researchers' own institutional inquiry identify a set of limitations that no single existing system has yet resolved. Prior computerized admission platforms — whether deployed in Philippine state universities and colleges or in foreign higher-education settings — tend to address isolated facets of the admissions pipeline: static information management through database-driven record keeping (Aguinaldo, 2021; Hassan et al., 2020), cloud-based student information systems that centralize enrollment data (Catimban & Doria, 2022; Olalla & Merabita, 2023), or single-purpose tools such as online application forms and result-query portals (Mendoza et al., 2022; Singh & Yadav, 2022). While these contributions advance digitalization in varying degrees, they have not addressed the operational conditions of a fully manual, paper-based admission testing workflow still found in many regional Philippine SUCs. Interview data from the partner institution confirm the depth of this disconnect: personnel in the Guidance Office score entrance examinations entirely by hand, requiring two to three working days to process approximately fifty applicants — a cycle that, when scaled to the institution's actual volume of three hundred to four hundred applicants per testing period, translates into weeks of cumulative delay [SIM-GUID-08]. No optical mark recognition (OMR) technology has ever been adopted, and the absence of any automated scoring mechanism perpetuates reliance on visual inspection and manual tallying [SIM-GUID-01]. Beyond procedural inefficiency, a structural problem persists: coordination between the Registrar's Office and the Guidance Office remains informal and undocumented, mediated primarily through ad hoc verbal or paper-based exchanges rather than through a shared, auditable system [SIM-REG-04]. Consequently, no audit trail exists to establish who accessed, modified, or transmitted applicant records at any stage of the process [SIM-GUID-05], and no institutional data-privacy protocol governs the handling of personally identifiable information collected during admission [SIM-REG-19]. From the applicant's perspective, the absence of any self-service capability means that examinees must physically return to the campus on a scheduled date merely to retrieve their results — a requirement that imposes disproportionate time and travel costs on prospective students from geographically dispersed barangays [SIM-REG-03]. The research gap, therefore, is that no existing study or deployed system adequately addresses the combined challenges of: (a) a fully manual admission testing process operating within a regional Philippine SUC, (b) the absence of automated scoring despite high applicant volumes of three hundred to four hundred per cycle, (c) the lack of inter-office coordination systems bridging the Registrar and Guidance offices, (d) the absence of audit trails and data-privacy safeguards, and (e) the unavailability of applicant-facing self-service capabilities. It is this combination of deficiencies that SecureCAT — a dynamic, real-time examination orchestration platform featuring offline-first architecture, automated OMR-based scoring, role-based access control, multi-office workflow coordination, an integrated AI companion, and comprehensive audit logging — is positioned to address.
