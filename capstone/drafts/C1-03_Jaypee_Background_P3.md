## Background of the Study (National Context)

[indent] The Philippine higher education system, overseen by the Commission on Higher Education (CHED), comprises over 200 State Universities and Colleges (SUCs) tasked with providing accessible tertiary education across the archipelago, yet the digital readiness of these institutions varies dramatically between metropolitan centers and regional campuses. The enactment of Republic Act No. 10931, or the Universal Access to Quality Tertiary Education Act, which eliminated tuition and miscellaneous fees at all SUCs, significantly increased applicant volumes at public institutions — a phenomenon confirmed at ISPSC Tagudin, where the Registrar Office processes approximately 300 to 400 applicants per admission cycle, a surge that strains already resource-constrained manual workflows and intensifies the need for digitized processing infrastructure (Malaluan & Wang, 2023). Concurrently, Republic Act No. 10173, the Data Privacy Act of 2012, imposes strict compliance requirements on all Philippine institutions that collect and process personal identifiable information, including educational establishments handling applicant records; however, as the ISPSC Registrar acknowledged during the institutional assessment, current data protection measures amount to little more than an operating system password and a locked filing cabinet, with no formal data privacy protocol in place — a gap the Campus Director described as non-negotiable to resolve, underscoring the urgency of building privacy-by-design principles into any proposed admission system (National Privacy Commission, 2024). While flagship SUCs such as the University of the Philippines and metropolitan institutions in Metro Manila have made substantial progress in deploying digital enrollment management systems, integrated student information portals, and cloud-based academic platforms, regional campuses like ISPSC Tagudin continue to operate on fragmented toolchains — Google Forms for initial application, Excel spreadsheets for tracking, and Word templates for document generation — reflecting a broader national digital divide within the public higher education sector (Caintic & Lahaylahay, 2024). The Philippine government, through the Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT) and the National Broadband Plan, has articulated an ambitious digitalization agenda aimed at modernizing public sector services, including education, yet the rollout of reliable broadband infrastructure to rural and provincial areas — particularly the Ilocos Region, where ISPSC Tagudin is located — remains incomplete, with campus internet outages occurring once or twice weekly during the rainy season and directly disrupting the digital processes upon which modernized admissions would depend (DICT, 2023). Post-pandemic analyses of Philippine higher education reveal that while COVID-19 accelerated digital transformation at well-resourced institutions, many regional SUCs were left with ad hoc, unsustainable workarounds rather than systemic digital infrastructure, and admission testing — a high-stakes, data-sensitive, time-bound process — was among the workflows least addressed by institutional digitization efforts (Comprendio & Canlas, 2025). This national implementation gap means that although policy frameworks mandate both universal access to education and rigorous data privacy compliance, the operational reality at regional SUCs is one in which legislative intent outpaces technological capacity: applicant volumes grow under RA 10931, data privacy obligations tighten under RA 10173, yet the admission testing infrastructure at campuses like ISPSC Tagudin remains predominantly manual, paper-based, and digitally unprotected — a condition that positions the development of a secure, role-based, and offline-resilient admission system not merely as a convenience improvement but as a compliance imperative aligned with national digitalization goals.
