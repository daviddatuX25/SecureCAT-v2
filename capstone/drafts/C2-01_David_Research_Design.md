## Research Design

This study employed a descriptive developmental research design, a hybrid methodology that combines descriptive inquiry with iterative system construction and validation. Descriptive research involves the systematic observation, documentation, and characterization of existing conditions without manipulation of variables (Siedlecki, 2020), while developmental research encompasses the design, creation, and evaluation of solutions grounded in the findings of that descriptive inquiry (Richey & Klein, 2014, as cited in Putra et al., 2022). This dual approach is particularly suited to applied information technology research where the objective is not only to understand an existing problem but to construct and assess a functional system that addresses it. The descriptive developmental paradigm thus supports a full research arc: from identifying real-world conditions, through engineering a response, to measuring the outcome against established benchmarks.

In the context of this study, the descriptive component was applied to observe, document, and analyze the existing manual admission testing processes, role-based coordination gaps, data integrity vulnerabilities, and infrastructure constraints at ISPSC Tagudin Campus — directly addressing Specific Objective 1 (Identify). The developmental component involved the iterative design, construction, and validation of SecureCAT using the AI-Driven Development Lifecycle (AIDLC) as the software model, wherein AI-assisted code generation was governed by rigorous human review, automated testing, and architectural oversight at every phase (Addla, 2026; Raja, 2025) — addressing Specific Objective 2 (Develop). Finally, the evaluation of the completed system's usability and perceived task workload through the System Usability Scale (SUS) and the NASA Task Load Index (NASA-TLX) constituted the descriptive-assessment phase — addressing Specific Objective 3 (Evaluate). This three-phase alignment — Identify, Develop, Evaluate — ensures that each specific objective is anchored to a named methodological component, producing a coherent and defensible research design appropriate for a descriptive developmental study in information technology.
