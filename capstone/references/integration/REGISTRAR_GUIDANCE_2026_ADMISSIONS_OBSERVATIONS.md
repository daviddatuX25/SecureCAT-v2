# Registrar and Guidance Admissions Observations Reference Document
**Academic Year:** SY 2026-2027
**Locale:** ISPSC Tagudin Campus

---

## 1. Admissions as an Enrollment Choice Gate
Prospective students frequently apply and sit for entrance examinations at multiple regional Higher Education Institutions (HEIs). In this competitive landscape, the responsiveness and speed of the admissions pipeline function as a critical enrollment gate. 
* **Operational Friction:** Under the current manual system, score reporting and routing take one to two weeks. This delay causes administrative anxiety and directly drives applicant attrition. 
* **Student Decision Dynamics:** Applicants who experience faster, more transparent processing at competing institutions tend to enroll there, resulting in a loss of qualified candidates for ISPSC Tagudin Campus.

## 2. Program Capacities and Quotas
Under the Universal Access to Quality Tertiary Education Act (Republic Act No. 10931), SUCs experience high applicant demand. However, physical and instructional resources are finite, requiring strict program capacity limits (quotas).
* **Capacity Constraints:** Target limits are established for key programs (e.g., BSIT, BSBA, BSED) based on physical classroom availability (room utilization schedules) and faculty loading parameters.
* **Coordination Gaps:** The Guidance Office coordinates admissions but lacks real-time visibility into these capacity limits. Consequently, counselors cannot easily check whether program slots are filled when advising applicants.

## 3. Dynamic Quota Allocation
Program capacities are not static; they fluctuate during the enrollment cycle based on inter-departmental adjustments and final registration headcounts.
* **Administrative Overhead:** Program Heads, the Registrar, and the Guidance Office must coordinate frequently to update available slots. 
* **Risk of Overallocation:** In the absence of a synchronized database, counselors run the risk of recommending programs that have already reached their capacity limits, leading to scheduling friction and applicant dissatisfaction.

## 4. Extensible Aptitude Profiling
Counselors need the ability to collect and analyze student parameters beyond the baseline entrance test scores and General Weighted Average (GWA).
* **Counseling Customization:** The Guidance Office requires the capacity to record secondary academic indicators, such as specific senior high school subject grades in Mathematics, English, and Science.
* **Machine Learning Extensibility:** Capturing these additional metrics enables the system's K-Means clustering algorithm to be refined over time, providing more granular and precise aptitude profiles to support counselor-led consultations.
